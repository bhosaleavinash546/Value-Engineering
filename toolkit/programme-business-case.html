Should we fund a value programme? Three years of savings against what the programme costs to run, with every assumption on show.

Coverage is the share of the cost base studied this year. Cost that is studied again should use the 3–5% rate. Typical ranges from the course: a first proper study finds 8–15% of the cost studied, established programmes 3–5% a year. Use your own evidence where you have it, and say where each number came from.

Step 2 · What it's worth

ResultYear 1Year 2Year 33 years
Cost studied = cost base × coverage
Savings found = cost studied × saving rate
Savings implemented = found × share implemented
Savings this year = new savings × in-year share + earlier years' savings
Net benefit = savings this year − running cost

Step 3 · The headline for the board

Return over three years (savings ÷ running cost)
Payback: when cumulative net benefit turns positive
The biggest risk to these numbers, and what would show it early

Implementation costs such as tooling and testing belong in each idea's own business case. Here, use the saving after those costs.

Worked example (in the Excel file): a £50M cost base; 20%, 40% and 60% of it studied in years 1–3; savings of 10%, 8% and 6% found on it; half of them implemented; running cost £150k, £200k and £250k. Savings that year: £250k, £900k, £1.75M. Net benefit over three years: £2.3M, a return of about 4.8 to 1. It pays for itself in year 1, but only just: £100k. This is lower than the 10:1 often quoted for a single study because it counts only half of the savings found, phases them in through the year and includes the cost of building the programme.

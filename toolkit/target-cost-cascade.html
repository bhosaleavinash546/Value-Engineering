Target-Cost Cascade

Start from what the market will pay and work down: the allowable cost for the product, then each system, then each part.

The one rule: the target cost is never exceeded. If a feature has to be added, its cost is saved somewhere else.
Worked example (Module 13): an EV with a net selling price of £29,900 and a 22% margin has an allowable cost of £23,322. The drive unit gets 9.8% (£2,286), and the motor 46% of that: a target of £1,052. The motor currently costs £1,214, a value gap of £162 (13%). Hairpin winding −£38, one housing casting instead of three −£61, magnet grade −£43, supplier gain-share on machining −£26: −£168 in total, with £6 going back to the reserve.

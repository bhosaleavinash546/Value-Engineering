6.1 · Choose the right first product

Whether a programme succeeds depends a lot on how the first study goes, so choose carefully. Score each candidate on five things:

  • Spend: yearly volume × unit cost is big enough to matter.
  • Design control: you're actually allowed to change it.
  • Life left: at least two more years of production to collect the savings.
  • Pain: margin pressure or warranty problems, so people want it fixed.
  • Data: a costed parts list exists, or can be built in two weeks.

The ideal first product is a fairly simple, high-volume one that your own engineers designed. The worst is your flagship product, with tooling that can't change and a chief engineer who'll defend every detail.

6.2 · Your 90-day plan

  • Days 1–30: set up: Name a sponsor and a facilitator. Choose the pilot product. Fix a dated starting cost with finance. Write the scope and a target based on evidence. Buy competitor samples. The core team takes the full VE Academy; everyone else involved takes this course.
  • Days 31–60: run the study: Run the 3–5 day workshop. Hold the decision meeting and sign the decision log. Give every approved idea an owner and a date. Set up the savings funnel.
  • Days 61–90: deliver and learn: Start the monthly funnel review. Get the quick wins into production. Report the first checked savings. Tell the story widely. Choose the next two products, and update design guidelines with what you learned.

6.3 · The numbers to watch

Start with four:

  • Checked savings in production (L4) against the target
  • Ideas implemented: the share of approved ideas that reach production. Remember that 40–60% usually don't.
  • Time to savings: months from approval to production
  • Average payback

As the programme grows, add two more:

  • Coverage: the share of product cost being actively studied (aim for at least 60%)
  • Cost visibility: the share of the parts list with a should-cost estimate (aim for at least 80%)

Key points for leaders

  • Choose a first product you can actually change and that people want fixed. The first win sets the tone.
  • Aim to have checked savings in production within 90 days, then choose the next products.
  • Measure results, not activity: checked savings, how many ideas are implemented, and how fast.

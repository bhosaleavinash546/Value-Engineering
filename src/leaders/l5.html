5.1 · VAVE is a team effort

Most cost is decided in design, but no single department has all the answers:

  • Design engineering: Why each feature is there, and what can safely change.
  • Manufacturing: What's hard to make, slow to assemble or wasteful.
  • Purchasing: Supplier costs, alternatives, and what suppliers could offer if asked.
  • Quality and service: What fails in use and what customers complain about.
  • Finance: Checked numbers, so the savings are believed.
  • Sales and marketing: What customers actually value and will pay for.
  • Key suppliers: Deep knowledge of their own materials and processes.

Why train everyone, not just a few experts? When everyone thinks in functions, ideas come up in everyday design reviews and supplier meetings, not only in workshops. A shared language also cuts arguments: "what job does this part do?" is a question everyone can answer. Samsung trained more than 1,000 engineers in TRIZ in 2004 alone and made it an expected skill for promotion, because a method only pays off when it becomes how people work.

Sources: The TRIZ Journal; Altshuller Institute case reports on Samsung's TRIZ programme.

5.2 · Why leaders need it most

Programmes rarely fail because the ideas are weak. They fail because nobody follows through. Following through needs people, time, decisions and money, and only leaders can provide them. That's why this course exists.

5.3 · Five things only leaders can do

  • 1 · Sponsor it visibly. Put your name on the study, agree the scope and attend the decision meeting.
  • 2 · Set targets based on evidence. Work back from the market price and the margin you need. Treat cost as a design requirement, as important as weight or performance.
  • 3 · Protect the team's time. Free people up for the full workshop, and fund the engineering time needed to make the changes afterwards.
  • 4 · Decide quickly. Every idea gets a yes, a no, or "more data by a date", recorded at the meeting. Remember that each month of delay costs savings.
  • 5 · Track real savings. Review the savings funnel every month. Only count savings that are in production and checked by finance. Reward those, and give credit to the people who came up with the ideas as well as those who carried them out.

Who should own the programme? Someone neutral. A programme run from engineering tends to ignore purchasing, and one run from purchasing ignores design. A finance or operations director as sponsor keeps both sides balanced.

5.4 · Why programmes stall, and what leaders do about it

What goes wrongWhat leaders do
  • No sponsor who can make decisions: Name one before the study starts.
  • A team from one department: Insist on a mixed team, including suppliers.
  • Savings targets without the people to deliver them: Fund the engineering time.
  • A great presentation, but no decisions: Require a signed decision log.
  • Savings claimed at approval, not in production: Count savings only at L4.
  • Measuring activity (studies run) instead of results: Measure checked savings.
  • Stopping after the first round: Keep going, because savings build up year after year.

Key points for leaders

  • VAVE needs every department. Train widely, so function thinking becomes everyday practice.
  • Most programmes fail on follow-through, and follow-through is a leadership job.
